What I actually do

Security tooling is a demanding place to write software. The datasets are enormous and high-cardinality, the queries are exploratory rather than predictable, and the person on the other end is an analyst halfway through an investigation with no patience for a slow filter or an ambiguous chart.

Getting that right means caring about query shape on the backend and render performance on the frontend at the same time, which is why I've never been much good at staying on one side of the stack. Most of my work sits at the seam: making a Go service fast enough that the interface in front of it can stay honest about what's happening in the data.

Before security I spent three years in consumer-scale web engineering — Next.js and Node at BYJU'S Exam Prep, React and Flutter client work at Moonraft. I came into all of this from a mechanical engineering degree and taught myself the rest.
